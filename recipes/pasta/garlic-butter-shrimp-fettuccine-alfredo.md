# Halcyon's Hearth — Garlic Butter Shrimp Fettuccine Alfredo

**Status:** Family-tested keeper — first cooked October 8, 2026. Verdict: **“It was PERFECT!”**

Fresh Buitoni fettuccine tossed with tender garlic-butter shrimp and a silky homemade Parmesan cream sauce, built in the same skillet with a small white-wine reduction and finished with fresh lemon and Italian parsley. Served with Caesar salad and garlic bread.

## Yield & Time

- **Serves:** About 2–3 generous portions
- **Time:** Approximately 30–35 minutes
- **Pasta:** Fresh/refrigerated Buitoni fettuccine (not dried); follow the package time, usually around 2–3 minutes. **Cook it last.**

## Cook-Along Method

Measurements are included in each step rather than repeated in a separate ingredient list.

### 1. Prep and heat the pasta water

1. Pat **1 lb raw, peeled and deveined shrimp** completely dry.
2. Season shrimp with **1/2 tsp regular paprika**, **1/2 tsp Italian seasoning**, **1/4 tsp freshly ground black pepper**, and **1/4 tsp salt** (adjust to taste).
3. Mince **4–5 garlic cloves**, chop **1–2 tbsp fresh flat-leaf Italian parsley**, grate **3/4–1 cup Parmesan**, and zest **about 1/2 tsp lemon peel** from a fresh lemon. Cut the lemon for juice.
4. Put a large pot of generously salted water on the stove to come to a boil; don't add the fresh pasta yet.

### 2. Sear the shrimp

1. Heat **1 tbsp olive oil** and **1 tbsp butter** in a wide skillet over medium-high heat.
2. Add shrimp in one layer; cook in batches if needed.
3. Sear approximately **1–2 minutes per side**, depending on size, until opaque and cooked through (145°F if checking with a thermometer).
4. Remove shrimp to a plate. Keep the browned flavorful bits in the pan.

### 3. Deglaze with white cooking wine

1. Reduce heat to medium and pour **2–3 tbsp white cooking wine** into the shrimp skillet.
2. Scrape up the browned bits and simmer **1–2 minutes**, until the liquid is mostly reduced.

**Note:** Commercial cooking wine can contain added salt, so season the finished sauce cautiously. Use white cooking wine here, not Marsala/masala.

### 4. Make garlic cream sauce

1. Reduce heat to medium-low; add **2 tbsp butter**.
2. Stir in the **4–5 minced garlic cloves** and **1/8–1/4 tsp crushed red pepper flakes** for about **30–45 seconds**, just until fragrant. Do not brown the garlic.
3. Pour in **1 cup heavy whipping cream** and stir.
4. Gently simmer **2–3 minutes** to meld the flavors.

### 5. Add Parmesan on gentle heat

1. Turn the burner to very low (or briefly off if the skillet runs hot).
2. Gradually stir in the **3/4–1 cup freshly grated Parmesan**, a little at a time, until the sauce is smooth.
3. Taste before adding salt; add pepper if needed.
4. Keep the sauce warm, without boiling, while the fresh pasta cooks.

**Key:** High heat after adding Parmesan can make Alfredo grainy or oily.

### 6. Cook the fresh fettuccine **last**

1. Once the sauce is ready, add **8–12 oz fresh Buitoni fettuccine** (or one package in that size range) to the boiling salted water.
2. Stir gently to separate the strands. Cook to the **package directions**; check early because fresh pasta often takes only about **2–3 minutes**.
3. **Reserve 1 cup starchy pasta water** before draining; don't rinse or oil the noodles.
4. Transfer immediately into the warm sauce.

### 7. Toss and finish

1. Toss the fettuccine in the sauce. Add reserved pasta water **1–2 tbsp at a time** until the sauce is glossy and coats the noodles.
2. Add the shrimp and any collected juices; toss gently just long enough to warm through.
3. Turn off the heat. Add **1/2 tsp fresh lemon zest**, **1/2 tsp fresh lemon juice to start**, and most of the **1–2 tbsp chopped parsley**.
4. Taste. Add a little more lemon only if needed; finish with remaining parsley, extra Parmesan if desired, and black pepper. Serve immediately.

## Why This Version Works

- **Fresh pasta at the very end:** avoids gummy, clumped noodles while the sauce is being made.
- **Shrimp cooked separately:** helps preserve a delicate, tender texture rather than boiling them in the Alfredo.
- **White-wine deglaze:** uses the flavorful seared bits from the skillet and adds depth.
- **Very gentle Parmesan heat + starchy pasta water:** makes a creamy sauce that clings to the noodles instead of breaking or turning gluey.
- **Lemon zest, minimal juice, fresh parsley:** brightens the rich cream and butter without overtaking the shrimp.
- **Regular paprika:** works beautifully here; smoked paprika is not required.
