# Halcyon's Creamy Tomato Pesto & Broccoli Fusilli

**Status:** First-cook success — October 28, 2026. Verdict: **"It's actually really damn good! The sauce is perfect, honestly."**

A satisfying, single-serving vegetarian pasta with fresh broccoli (stems included), tomato pesto, cream cheese, and dried grated Parmesan. The creamy orange-red sauce clings to the fusilli without heavy cream. **Preserve the original sauce proportions** — no adjustments needed to the first-cook result.

## Yield & Timing

- **Yield:** 1 generous serving
- **Time:** About 20–25 minutes
- **Equipment:** Pot, colander, spoon
- **Pasta:** Dry fusilli
- **Broccoli:** Fresh; tender stems as well as florets

**Measurement honesty:** The proportions below are those given during the cook-along; the cook confirmed the finished sauce was perfect but did not separately report measured amounts or any deviations. Keep these as the reference recipe, not independently verified measurements.

## Ingredients

- **3 oz dry fusilli** (about 85 g)
- **1½–2 cups fresh broccoli**, florets plus peeled/sliced tender stems
- **2 tbsp tomato pesto**
- **2 tbsp cream cheese**
- **1–2 tbsp dried grated Parmesan**
- **1 garlic clove**, minced (or ¼ tsp garlic powder)
- **¼–½ cup reserved pasta water**, added gradually as needed
- **¼ tsp black pepper**, or to taste
- **¼ tsp Italian seasoning** (optional)
- Salt for pasta water; season finished dish only if needed

## Method — Amounts Included in Each Step

1. **Prepare the broccoli:** Rinse **1½–2 cups fresh broccoli**. Cut florets into bite-size pieces; trim the woody bottom of the stalk and peel the tough outer skin from thick stems. Slice tender stems into thin coins or small pieces so they cook evenly.
2. **Cook the pasta:** Bring a pot of salted water to a boil. Add **3 oz dry fusilli** and cook according to the package directions.
3. **Add the stems first:** With **4–5 minutes** left in the pasta cooking time, add the sliced broccoli stems to the pasta pot.
4. **Add the florets:** With **2–3 minutes** left, add the broccoli florets. The goal is tender broccoli that still has some structure. Before draining, **reserve ½ cup starchy pasta water**. Drain pasta and broccoli together.
5. **Warm the pesto and garlic:** Return the empty pot to **low heat**. Add **2 tbsp tomato pesto**, **1 minced garlic clove** (or ¼ tsp garlic powder), **¼ tsp black pepper**, and **¼ tsp Italian seasoning if using**. Stir **30–45 seconds** until fragrant; do not brown the garlic.
6. **Build the creamy sauce:** **Turn the heat off**. Add **2 tbsp cream cheese** and **¼ cup hot reserved pasta water**; stir until creamy and smooth. Use very low heat briefly only if the cream cheese needs help melting.
7. **Finish with Parmesan:** Stir in **1–2 tbsp dried grated Parmesan** off heat. Avoid boiling, which can make dried Parmesan grainy.
8. **Toss:** Return the drained fusilli and broccoli to the pot. Toss thoroughly to coat. Add more reserved pasta water **1 tbsp at a time**, up to about **½ cup total**, *only if needed*. **Do not automatically thin the sauce**: the first-cook sauce was rated perfect as served.
9. **Rest and serve:** Taste before adding salt (pesto and Parmesan are already salty). Let sit **1–2 minutes**, then serve.

## Why It Works

- **Cream cheese + tomato pesto + starchy pasta water:** creates a thick sauce that clings to the spirals, without heavy cream.
- **Dried Parmesan off heat:** adds savory depth without risking graininess from overheating.
- **Staggered broccoli cooking:** stems go in before florets, keeping both pleasant to eat.
- **Fresh broccoli volume:** makes this a substantial dinner with fiber and vegetables; no need to make every meal a strict high-protein meal.
