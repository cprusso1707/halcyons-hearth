# Dark Mole Poblano

A small-batch, mole poblano-style chicken mole built for the flavor profile that matters here: **dark, earthy, rich, deeply chile-forward, and Mexican-chocolatey without becoming dessert**.

> Traditional mole varies widely by region and family. This is not presented as the one canonical recipe; it follows the traditional architecture of dried chiles, nuts/seeds, toasted starch, tomato, warm spices, chocolate, and broth.

## Yield

About 3–4 servings.

## Ingredients

### Chiles
- 2 ancho chiles
- 2 guajillo chiles
- 1 pasilla chile

### Body
- 2 Tbsp sesame seeds
- 2 Tbsp almonds or peanuts
- 1 small corn tortilla
- 1 small tomato
- 1/4 onion
- 2 garlic cloves

### Spices
- 1/2 tsp Mexican oregano
- 1/4 tsp cumin
- 1/4 tsp cinnamon
- Tiny pinch ground clove
- Tiny pinch black pepper

### Dark Magic
- 1–1 1/2 oz Mexican chocolate, such as Ibarra or Abuelita
- 1 tsp brown sugar, only if needed
- 2 cups chicken broth
- Salt, to taste
- Neutral oil

### Chicken
- 4 chicken thighs

## Method

### 1. Toast and soak the chiles
Stem and seed the ancho, guajillo, and pasilla chiles.

Toast them briefly in a dry skillet, about **10 seconds per side**, just until fragrant and pliable. Do not let them burn; burned dried chiles turn sharply bitter.

Transfer the toasted chiles to a bowl, cover with hot water, and soak for **15–20 minutes**.

### 2. Toast the body ingredients
In the same skillet:

1. Toast the sesame seeds and nuts until fragrant, then remove them.
2. Toast the tortilla until it develops **quite dark spots**. A little intentional bitterness helps create the deep, earthy mole flavor.
3. Char the tomato, onion, and garlic until browned around the edges.

### 3. Blend
Drain the softened chiles.

Blend together:

- soaked chiles
- tomato
- onion
- garlic
- sesame seeds
- nuts
- toasted tortilla
- oregano
- cumin
- cinnamon
- clove
- black pepper
- about 1 cup chicken broth

Blend until **extremely smooth**. Mole benefits from being blended longer than seems necessary.

### 4. Fry the mole base
Heat about **2 Tbsp neutral oil** in a heavy pot over medium-low heat.

Carefully pour in the puree. It may spit.

Cook for **10–15 minutes**, stirring frequently, until the sauce becomes darker and thicker and the raw chile aroma cooks away.

### 5. Simmer
Gradually add the remaining chicken broth until the mole reaches a thick but flowing sauce consistency.

Simmer gently for **20–30 minutes**, stirring periodically.

### 6. Add the chocolate
Stir in **1 oz Mexican chocolate** first and let it fully dissolve.

Taste before adding more.

The finished flavor should unfold roughly like this:

**chile → toasted earthiness → nuts and warm spice → subtle sweetness → chocolate at the finish**

The chocolate should deepen and round the sauce, not make it taste like chocolate syrup.

### 7. Balance
Adjust carefully:

- **Bitter:** add up to 1 tsp brown sugar.
- **Flat:** add salt before adding sugar.
- **Too chocolate-forward:** loosen with broth and let the chile flavors lead again.
- **Too thick:** add broth a little at a time.

### 8. Cook the chicken
Brown the chicken thighs separately, then add them to the mole.

Simmer gently in the sauce for about **20 minutes**, or until tender and fully cooked.

## For an Extra-Dark, Earthy Mole

For the specific **dark, earthy, rich, Mexican-chocolatey** profile:

- Lean on the **ancho and pasilla** for depth.
- Use the **guajillo** mainly for brightness and red-fruit chile character.
- Toast the tortilla fairly dark without burning it.
- Keep the chocolate restrained enough that it remains part of the background architecture.
- Let the finished mole simmer long enough for the flavors to stop tasting like separate ingredients.

## Serving

Serve with rice and warm tortillas. A light sprinkle of toasted sesame seeds on top is traditional and works beautifully.
