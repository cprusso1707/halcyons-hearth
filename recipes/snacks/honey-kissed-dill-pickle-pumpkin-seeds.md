# Halcyon's Hearth — Honey-Kissed Dill Pickle Pumpkin Seeds

**Status:** **EXPERIMENT — UNTESTED.** Proposed October 30, 2026, for a future Halloween cook. This recipe intentionally lives on the `testing` branch, **not `main`**, until an actual batch earns a verdict.

A savory, herbaceous, tangy dill-pickle take on roasted jack-o'-lantern seeds, with **just a whisper of honey** to help the seasoning cling and round the acidity. The goal is **crisp and tangy, not sweet or sticky**.

## Hypothesis

A mere **¼ tsp honey per cup of seeds**, added *late* in the roast, will help the dill seasoning adhere without making an obvious honey glaze. Olive oil supplies most of the coating; vinegar powder or a very small amount of citric acid supplies the pickle bite.

**Risks:** Honey can scorch and remain tacky, dried dill can lose its aroma if baked too long, and too much acid can overpower the pumpkin seed. The quantities and timing below are **starting points, not verified results**.

## Yield & Equipment

- **Yield:** About 1 cup roasted seeds.
- **Oven:** 300°F to start, for gentler roasting with honey.
- **Estimated total roast:** 30–45 minutes depending on seed size and dryness; follow the texture more than the clock.
- **Equipment:** Colander, clean kitchen towel, mixing bowl, sheet pan, spatula.
- **Cooling:** 10 minutes before the final crunch/stickiness test.

## Ingredients — Proposed Test Batch

- 1 cup freshly cleaned pumpkin seeds (in their shells)
- 1 tsp olive oil
- **¼ tsp honey** — the intentional *whisper*
- ½ tsp dried dill weed
- ¼ tsp garlic powder
- ¼ tsp onion powder
- ¼ tsp kosher salt (adjust after tasting; especially if vinegar powder contains salt)
- **For pickle tang, choose one:**
  - A small pinch (around 1/16 tsp or less) of **citric acid**, added after roasting; **or**
  - A pinch of **vinegar powder**, added after roasting, checking its label for added salt
- Optional, for a second test only: a pinch of black pepper or additional dill after cooling

**Do not substitute liquid vinegar directly into the oven seasoning without adjusting the process:** its extra water may interfere with crisping.

## Method — First Proposed Trial

1. **Clean and dry:** Rinse seeds, remove strings, pat very dry and ideally air-dry another **10–15 minutes**. Skip boiling for the first trial so it is comparable to the successful garlic-Parmesan batch.
2. **Preheat:** Heat oven to **300°F**.
3. **Base coat:** Toss **1 cup seeds** with **1 tsp olive oil**, **¼ tsp garlic powder**, **¼ tsp onion powder**, and **¼ tsp kosher salt**. Hold the dill, honey and acid back.
4. **Initial roast:** Spread in a single layer. Roast about **20–30 minutes**, stirring every 10 minutes, until the shells are looking dry and starting to toast. This timing is only a checkpoint, not a guarantee of doneness.
5. **Honey + dill finish:** Gently warm **¼ tsp honey** until thin enough to drizzle. Toss the hot, oil-coated seeds thoroughly with the honey and **½ tsp dried dill weed** to distribute the tiny amount evenly. Return to the sheet in a single layer.
6. **Finish carefully:** Roast about **5–10 more minutes**, checking frequently for excessive browning or sticky clumps. If still soft, continue in short increments, watching the honey closely. Do **not** turn up the temperature to force browning.
7. **Add tang off heat:** Let the seeds cool a minute or two, then dust with a *tiny pinch* of citric acid **or** vinegar powder and toss. Taste before adding more: acid is easy to overdo.
8. **Cool and judge:** Cool **10 minutes** and assess crispness, coating adhesion, dill aroma, sourness, salt and sweetness.

- Whether dill survived the oven or needs to be added mostly after baking.
- Whether acid tasted like **dill pickle** instead of just a sour seed.
- Actual oven time and **crunch after cooling**.
- Family taste-test verdict and any modifications.
