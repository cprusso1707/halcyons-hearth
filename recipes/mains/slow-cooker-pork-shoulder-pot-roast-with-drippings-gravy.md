# Halcyon's Hearth — Slow-Cooker Pork Shoulder Pot Roast with Drippings Gravy

**Status:** First cook in progress — October 29, 2026. Seasoned, seared pork was photographed in the crock pot over red potatoes; **final tenderness, cooking time, flavor, and gravy results have not yet been reported**. Not yet rated a tested keeper.

A traditional, savory pork pot roast — **not pulled pork** — with bone-in pork shoulder, baby red potatoes, carrots, onion, garlic, herbs, and a brown gravy made from the slow-cooker juices. The goal is tender pork in thick slices or chunks, rather than deliberately shredded meat.

## Yield, Equipment & Timing

- **Pork:** Approximately **2.4 lb bone-in pork shoulder blade roast** with a small bone.
- **Yield:** About 3–4 servings.
- **Equipment:** Crock pot, large skillet (cast iron preferred), tongs, meat thermometer, small saucepan, whisk.
- **Cook setting chosen:** **HIGH**, estimated **4–5 hours**; start checking around **3½–4 hours**. LOW alternative: roughly 6–8 hours.
- **Rest:** 10–15 minutes.
- **Important:** Shoulder has connective tissue and may become shreddable by the time it becomes tender. Aim for chunks/slices that hold together, but do not promise clean roast-beef-style slices.

The measurements below are from the cook-along recipe, **not independently confirmed as used in exact quantities**. The photographed browned sear and the plan to use HIGH are confirmed.

## Ingredients — Roast and Vegetables

- **1 bone-in pork shoulder blade roast**, approximately **2.4 lb**
- **1–1½ lb baby red potatoes**, whole if small, halved if larger (skins on)
- **4–5 carrots**, cut into thick 2-inch sections
- **1 large onion**, cut into thick wedges
- **3–4 garlic cloves**, minced
- **1 tbsp olive oil**, for searing
- **1 tbsp butter**, added near the end of searing

### Seasoning

- **1½ tsp kosher salt** (use less if substituting fine table salt)
- **1 tsp black pepper**
- **1 tsp garlic powder**
- **1 tsp smoked paprika**
- **1 tsp dried thyme**
- **½ tsp dried rosemary**
- **½ tsp onion powder**

### Cooking Liquid

- **¾ cup beef or chicken broth**
- **1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce**
- **1 tsp Dijon mustard** (optional)
- **1 tbsp tomato paste** (optional)

### Drippings Gravy

- **1–1½ cups slow-cooker cooking juices**, strained if desired and excess surface fat skimmed off
- **1 tbsp cornstarch** plus **2 tbsp cold water** (slurry; start with less if there is not much liquid)
- **Extra unsalted broth**, only if needed to bring juices to about 1–1½ cups
- **1 tsp butter**, optional, to finish
- Black pepper and salt **to taste after reducing/thickening**

## Method — Roast

### 1. Prepare and season

1. Pat the **2.4 lb pork shoulder** dry.
2. Mix the **1½ tsp kosher salt, 1 tsp pepper, 1 tsp garlic powder, 1 tsp smoked paprika, 1 tsp thyme, ½ tsp rosemary, and ½ tsp onion powder**.
3. Rub the seasoning over the entire roast, including the fat cap. Let sit about **10–15 minutes** while preparing vegetables.

### 2. Sear the pork

1. Heat a large skillet over **medium-high heat** with **1 tbsp olive oil**.
2. Sear the broad sides of the seasoned pork for roughly **3–4 minutes each**, plus edges as practical, until well browned. Lower heat if the spices begin to scorch.
3. Add **1 tbsp butter** near the end and spoon the hot butter over the meat.
4. Keep the brown skillet bits for the cooking liquid.

**Photographed first-cook note:** The pork was visibly well browned with deep golden surfaces, darker caramelized patches, and rosemary before going into the slow cooker. The user showed red potatoes underneath the roast.

### 3. Load the crock pot

1. Arrange the **1 onion (wedges)**, **1–1½ lb baby red potatoes**, **4–5 thick-cut carrots**, and **3–4 minced garlic cloves** in the slow cooker.
2. Place the seared pork on top, ideally **fat-side up**.
3. Verify that the slow-cooker lid closes fully.

### 4. Add the savory liquid

1. Into the searing skillet, pour **¾ cup beef or chicken broth** and scrape up the browned bits.
2. Stir in **1 tbsp Worcestershire**, plus **1 tsp Dijon** and **1 tbsp tomato paste** if using.
3. Pour around the pork rather than trying to submerge it. The vegetables and roast will release more juices while cooking.

### 5. Slow-cook on HIGH

1. Cover and cook on **HIGH**, with the first tenderness check around **3½–4 hours**; a **4–5-hour** total is a starting estimate, not a guarantee.
2. Check the center of the pork away from the small bone with a food thermometer. **145°F with a 3-minute rest is the USDA minimum for intact pork roasts**, but pork shoulder is generally tough at that point. For pot-roast tenderness, it often needs to reach approximately **185–195°F or higher**, judged by a fork sliding in easily.
3. If tough, continue in **20–30-minute increments**. Stop when it is tender enough to portion but still largely holding together. Keep the lid closed between checks.
4. Check potatoes and carrots are fully tender.
5. Move meat to a platter and rest **10–15 minutes**. Remove the small bone and slice thickly or cut into generous chunks. Lift out vegetables with a slotted spoon; reserve the cooking juices for gravy.

## Make the Brown Gravy from the Juices

1. Carefully ladle or pour out the hot slow-cooker juices, allowing the fat to rise. Skim off excess fat; **keep the savory juices**. Strain if a smoother gravy is desired.
2. Measure **1–1½ cups drippings** into a saucepan. If short, top up with a little **unsalted beef or chicken broth**.
3. Bring to a gentle simmer over **medium heat**. Taste first: the juices may already be salty, so **do not add salt yet**.
4. In a separate cup, whisk **1 tbsp cornstarch** with **2 tbsp cold water** until smooth.
5. Whisk in about half the slurry and simmer for **1–2 minutes**. Add more slurry gradually if needed; use all of it only if the gravy still needs thickening.
6. For a richer finish, turn off the heat and whisk in **1 tsp butter**. Add black pepper and salt to taste.
7. Spoon generously over the rested pork, red potatoes, carrots, and onions.

**Texture adjustment:** A thicker gravy can use a little more cornstarch slurry, mixed cold first. If too thick, whisk in broth a tablespoon or two at a time. If drippings seem greasy, skim more fat before thickening rather than adding extra starch.

## Why It Works

- **Pork shoulder marbling and connective tissue** become rich and tender under moist, slow heat.
- **Searing** builds deeper roasted flavor for both pork and gravy.
- **Vegetable bed** keeps the meat over the potatoes, carrots, and onions as juices circulate.
- **A modest amount of broth** prevents a washed-out pot-roast flavor; the meat and vegetables supply additional liquid.
- **Worcestershire, herbs, and optional Dijon/tomato paste** create a savory roast profile rather than barbecue or pulled-pork seasoning.
- **Cornstarch-thickened drippings** make an easy gravy without masking the roast's flavor.
